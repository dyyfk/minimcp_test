\subsection{Internal accuracy and timing cost}
\label{sec:live}
\label{sec:liveloop}
\label{sec:livecurve}
The upper MiniCPM block of Table~\ref{tab:transfer} reports
answer-content accuracy on the fixed 240-query internal test set:
40.8\% locally, then 46.7\%, 54.2\%, and 62.9\% for conservative,
balanced, and aggressive. Their realized escalation rates are
8.3\%, 25.0\%, and 51.7\% (Appendix~\ref{app:transferdetail}).
Aggressive exceeds the table's random reference of 56.1\% by
6.8 percentage points. Per-tier internal comparisons are in
Table~\ref{tab:live}. Aggressive recovers 74.6\% of the measured
local-to-always accuracy gap while using 51.7\% expert calls.
This measures selection value relative to the recorded endpoints.

Gains vary across the fixed internal strata: factual QA improves by
52.5 points, while math accuracy is unchanged. Of 60 knowledge
questions, 43 exceed the expert's input window. These descriptive
results identify an input constraint and task differences, without
attributing the accuracy gap to truncation or changing the test
mixture (Appendix~\ref{app:internal-strata}).

The timing diagnostic makes the cost visible
(Figure~\ref{fig:dualview}). Internal mean time increases from 5.7\,s
locally to 15.3\,s at balanced and 27.3\,s at aggressive; aggressive
P95 is 103.2\,s. The always arm averages 45.1\,s. A lower call rate
can reduce expert-path exposure, but these tails do not support
calling the current aggressive setting low latency. An earlier
cached-outcome sweep suggested nominal 15/25/40\% as candidates;
those settings require new runs and are not substituted for the
measured 15/30/50\% recipe here (Appendix~\ref{app:current-protocol}).

Table~\ref{tab:transfer}'s measured always arm and expert-text row
score different objects. On the 238 internal always-arm rows with expert scores,
returned text reaches 76.5\% and intended relay content reaches
71.0\%; the full 240-row always arm scores 70.4\%. This paired
5.5-point gap combines relay content changes with judge variability;
it does not isolate formatting loss or acoustic intelligibility.
The table's ``relay-free bound'' is a returned-text reference, not a
proven upper bound on every routed or spoken answer.
