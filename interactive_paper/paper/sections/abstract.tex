\begin{abstract}
A real-time duplex speech model should answer easy questions immediately, but
hard questions often benefit from waiting for stronger reasoning. The challenge
is to choose between these paths at the moment the model begins to speak.
Recent systems learn related mode decisions through special-token generation
inside the dialogue model, coupling routing to turn-taking and temporally
annotated full-duplex training. We study a post-training alternative. We freeze
MiniCPM-o~4.5 and fit a logistic failure readout on hidden states already
available at its native listen-to-speak transition. The resulting gate requires
no additional model generation and exposes thresholds that can be retuned
without changing the duplex policy. The signal learned from text transfers to
unseen human speech, while native-state aggregation improves its reliability.
On the internal benchmark, selective handoff improves answer accuracy by 22.1
points. Compared with always using the expert, it reduces mean server time to
first answer audio by 35.3 percent. It also outperforms matched random routing
across external speech question-answering pools and transfers to a second
duplex model family. These results show that an existing duplex model can be
equipped to answer easy turns immediately and wait for stronger reasoning on
harder ones, without another round of duplex training.
\end{abstract}
