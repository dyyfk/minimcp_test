\begin{abstract}
Real-time speech interfaces must respond within a turn-taking deadline
of a few hundred milliseconds, yet some user queries exceed what the
deployed speech model can answer reliably. Selective escalation to a
stronger expert is well studied for text, but speech tightens the
requirement: the decision must arrive before the model commits to a
response. We ask whether a speech model's hidden states expose a
competence signal early enough for this handoff, and whether that
signal survives distribution and modality shift. On MiniCPM-o~4.5, we
compare decode-time uncertainty, verbalized self-assessment, and linear
probes across layers, using leave-one-query-pool-out evaluation,
matched text and speech inputs, and an end-to-end escalation loop.
Under distribution shift the conventional final-layer probe collapses
(AUC 0.822 in distribution, 0.372 on held-out math), while the same
read at a mid-network layer remains reliable (AUC 0.931), transfers
from text calibration to speech input, and is available at the end of
the user turn, roughly 30\,ms before the first output token. Gating on
this signal in the live system raises end-to-end answer accuracy from
42.2\% to 63.3\% while escalating 57\% of turns, and the same gate,
with its layer and features frozen, improves accuracy on all five
external speech-QA benchmarks over always-local operation. These
results identify a mid-network representation, rather than the
conventional final-layer read, as a practical competence signal for
real-time escalation.
\end{abstract}
