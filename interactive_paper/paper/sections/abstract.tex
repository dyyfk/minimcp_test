\begin{abstract}
A speech model's decision to speak does not establish that it can answer
correctly. We study whether its frozen hidden states expose a useful
signal for selective escalation to a stronger expert. On MiniCPM-o~4.5,
a final-layer, last-token failure probe inverts on held-out math queries
with text input (AUC 0.372), whereas the same read at layer 22 reaches
0.931. A text-calibrated mid-layer probe also transfers to speech-input
states (AUC 0.855). We use this read location in a separately calibrated
answer-onset gate and evaluate its decisions in the model's native
listen/speak protocol. On 240 internal test queries, answer-content
accuracy increases from 40.8\% to 62.9\% at 51.7\% escalation, exceeding
the reported random-mixture reference by 6.8 points. Across five
external speech-QA pools, the aggressive tier improves macro accuracy
from 59.4\% to 80.9\%, versus 75.2\% for the reference. Reconstructed
timing diagnostics expose substantial expert-path costs; these experiments
do not establish an end-to-end conversational latency benefit. The
results support mid-layer competence readout as a routing signal and
identify the timing, calibration, and relay constraints that must be
resolved for real-time use.
\end{abstract}
