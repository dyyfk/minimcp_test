\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The central design choice is separation, not a new way to train a duplex
model. A learned Listen/Speak or \texttt{[THINK]} token is useful when the
desired policy and training distribution are fixed. A post-training readout is
useful when the interaction checkpoint should remain unchanged and the cost of
waiting or calling an expert may vary after deployment. Because the readout
uses answer-onset states already produced by the model, the marginal local
computation is small; because the threshold is external, the operating point
can change without modifying generation.

The results also distinguish floor readiness from answer readiness. The native
model's decision to speak supplies a well-timed intervention point, but a
separate competence signal is needed to decide whether taking that turn is
wise. Mid-layer transfer and native aggregation are two complementary ways of
preserving that signal under distribution and modality shift.

Our evidence covers two duplex model families and primarily single-turn speech
QA. The gate uses in-regime labeled calibration, and TTFA ends at server PCM
readiness. Multi-turn continuity and human-rated interaction quality remain
important next evaluations.
