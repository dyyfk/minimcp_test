\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Spoken interaction places a tight deadline on response selection
\citep{stivers2009universals}. A local speech model can maintain the
conversation, while a stronger expert handles difficult questions.
Calling the expert on every turn increases cost and waiting time;
selective escalation instead requires an early estimate of whether the
local answer will fail. A duplex model already decides when to speak,
but this floor-management decision need not reveal whether it knows
the answer.

Hidden-state probes and self-evaluation can estimate correctness in
text models \citep{kadavath2022language,azaria2023internal,kossen2024semantic},
and text routers allocate queries between models
\citep{ong2024routellm,varshney2026llmrouter}. Applying these ideas to
speech introduces three questions. Does a readout distinguish failures
on unseen query pools? Does it survive the move from text to audio
input? And where in the streaming protocol is the state actually
available? High in-distribution AUC or a fast offline probe alone does
not answer these questions.

We investigate them on frozen MiniCPM-o~4.5
\citep{openbmb2026minicpmo}, with raw-backbone and speech-model controls.
The representational result is input- and position-specific: on text
input, a final-layer, last-token probe falls below chance on held-out
math, while a mid-layer probe remains predictive. The final-layer
failure does not generalize to audio-calibrated, audio-input probes.
The practical motivation for the middle layer is therefore robustness
across the tested conditions, rather than a universal absence of
competence information at the output.

Our contributions are threefold. First, a layer and position audit
shows that LOPO math AUC increases from 0.372 at the final layer to
0.931 at layer 22; matched controls constrain, but do not identify, the
cause of the late-layer degradation. Second, a text-calibrated
mid-layer probe transfers to speech-input states at AUC 0.855,
separating representation transfer from the additional calibration
needed for native serving. Third, a native-protocol benchmark shows
that a separately fitted answer-onset gate selects useful expert
calls: it improves internal answer-content accuracy by 22.1 points
and exceeds the reported random-mixture reference by 6.8 points at the
aggressive tier. We report accuracy together with the recorded timing
costs and distinguish this benchmark from a causal streaming evaluation
of audible responses.
