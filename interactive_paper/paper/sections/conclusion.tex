\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Real-time speech agents should neither delay every easy turn nor answer hard
turns without sufficient reasoning. We show that this choice need not be
learned as another generated control token. Failure signals remain readable in
frozen duplex-model states, transfer to unseen human speech, and enable expert
handoff at the native speak boundary of a live conversational pipeline.

The architecture keeps fast interaction intact while moving competence
arbitration into a lightweight, tunable serving layer. This yields an adjustable
accuracy--latency frontier rather than a fixed think-or-talk behavior. Full-duplex
control should govern both \emph{when the agent has the floor} and \emph{when it
should trust itself enough to use it during an ongoing real-time interaction}.
