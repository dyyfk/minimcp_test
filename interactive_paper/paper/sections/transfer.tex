\subsection{External transfer and its limits}
\label{sec:transfer}
\label{sec:transfer-neg}
The native gate's weights, feature definition, layer, and per-language
thresholds stay fixed across the four external English QA pools. The
\emph{avg} column excludes the internal set and $\Delta$ is its gain over
always-local. Aggressive macro accuracy rises from \textbf{61.5\% to
82.9\%}, compared with 77.1\% for the matched random policy: +21.4 points
over local answering and +5.9 over random. Realized call rates adapt to
the pool, from 18.8\% on Llama Questions to 66.0\% on SD-QA. TriviaQA and
SD-QA show the largest gains over local answering (+25.6 and +34.5 points)
and over their random references (+6.9 and +8.8 points). The lower-budget
tiers make smaller changes, as expected when the expert is reserved for
only the highest-risk tail.

Failure categories and later reads help explain the boundary. In the
native failure-type audit, specific-but-wrong answers comprise
70\% of failures and are ranked at AUC 0.81, while execution slips
in multi-step reasoning are near chance (0.47). In a separate
read-time sweep, external mean AUC increases from 0.755 at commitment
to 0.791 after two answer chunks. Later chunks expose useful failure
information without identifying the mechanism underlying this change
(Appendix~\ref{app:readtime}).

The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:transfer} provides a portability check on
NemotronLabs-VoiceChat-11B. Its three-layer answer-onset readout reaches 0.818
out-of-fold and 0.816 mean external AUC. At a 50\% cached replay budget,
external macro accuracy rises from 43.8\% to 75.7\%, versus 67.0\% for matched
random routing. All tiers appear in the table; Appendix~\ref{app:nvda}
gives replay details.
