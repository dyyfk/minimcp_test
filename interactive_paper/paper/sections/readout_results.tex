\subsection{Layer and position matter}
\label{sec:duplex}
We first evaluate the read location independently of the expert path.
A layer-by-position sweep under leave-one-pool-out (LOPO) transfer
localizes the text-input failure to late last-token states
(Figure~\ref{fig:layersweep}). A last-token probe at L22 reaches
\textbf{0.931} AUC on held-out math, compared with the final-layer
baseline of 0.372, while mean pooling reduces the
late-layer degradation. A raw Qwen backbone, subsampled to match
per-pool failure rates, remains near 0.96; the Qwen2.5 control family
shows a similar ordering (Table~\ref{tab:lopo}). These comparisons
associate the degradation with the tested fine-tuned checkpoints,
without isolating a causal training objective.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{layer_sweep}
  \caption{\textbf{Text-input transfer depends on layer and position.}
  LOPO math AUC across layers: late last-token reads deteriorate on
  the duplex checkpoints, while mid-layer reads and raw-backbone
  controls remain predictive. Layer 22 is the MiniCPM-o~4.5 read
  location used for the native gate. This plot does not show an
  equivalent collapse under audio calibration and audio input.}
  \label{fig:layersweep}
\end{figure}

With audio calibration and audio input, the final-layer probe reaches
0.936 on LOPO math. We retain L22 for robustness across the tested
conditions; the mechanism behind the text-input degradation remains
open (Appendix~\ref{app:mech}).

At native answer onset, we separate the effect of layer from the
effect of combining positions. All four readouts use one audio batch
with identical training pools, labels, splits, and head recipe
(Figure~\ref{fig:native-ablation}). At L22, aggregation raises mean
failure-prediction AUC from 0.595 to 0.695; at the final layer, the
change is smaller (0.654 to 0.662). The single-state L22 read has no
advantage over the final layer. Thus, the evidence supports using
aggregation with L22, rather than attributing the native signal to
middle-layer selection alone. Named per-pool results and cached
routing gains are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:native-ablation}.

  \centering

  \caption{\textbf{Aggregation improves failure prediction at L22.}
  Mean AUC across five external pools under leave-one-pool-out

  tail state; blue bars concatenate that state with the tail mean
  and input mean (\S\ref{sec:readlocation}). Arrows show the AUC

\end{figure}

\subsection{Text-to-speech representation transfer}
\label{sec:xmodal}
A text-calibrated L22 probe evaluated on speech-input states reaches
\textbf{0.855} AUC. Figure~\ref{fig:xmodal-transfer} (left) locates
where this readability emerges: probes trained on text-input hidden
states and scored on speech-input states for the same queries are
near chance in early layers and stabilize from roughly the middle of
the network on every end-to-end-trained model --- MiniCPM-o~4.5
(plateau 0.82--0.87), MiniCPM-o~2.6 (0.74--0.80, onset
$\sim$46\% depth), and the cross-family Qwen2.5-Omni control
(0.80--0.83, onset $\sim$25\% depth). The frozen-backbone
Freeze-Omni adapter control never leaves the 0.52--0.60 band on
identical backbone weights: the modality-shared mid-layer
representation is created by training the backbone on the modality,
not by the adapter alone. The two panels answer different questions
and we keep them distinct: the left panel is \emph{matched-query}
modality transfer (same 600 queries as text and as TTS audio); the
right panel is the strictly harder \emph{query-disjoint} test ---
probes fitted on the calibration pool, evaluated on 200 held-out
SD-QA human recordings --- where the text-calibrated read on real
speech reaches 0.760 at the deployed layer (band 0.76--0.80 over
L22--L26), confirming the recipe is neither a TTS artifact nor
memorization of the calibration questions (full per-layer tables in
Appendix~\ref{app:signal}). These results concern
representation transfer. The escalation experiments below use a
separately fitted native probe with three L22 positions, as defined
in \S\ref{sec:readlocation}.

  \centering

  end-to-end-trained models and transfers to real speech.} Left:
